# Personality Injection: Before → After (Invoice Yarn CEO)

From a session where user wanted to spicen up a CEO SOUL.md.  
The original had solid strategic substance but read like a corporate style guide.  
The rewrite preserved the operating principles and injected real human energy into the voice.

## The Core Insight

**Keep strategic bones intact if they're earned. Replace the voice section with a pulse.**

## Strategy: The Layered Rewrite

1. Audited what was working: the Operating Stance section was tight — real CEO decision frameworks.
2. Identified what was flat: the Voice & Tone section read like a grammar guide (rules about exclamation points, "use not utilize").
3. Solution: kept Operating Stance nearly verbatim, rewrote Voice & Vibe to sound like a human who's fired up about building something that matters.

## Before (Voice section - flat/corporate)

```
Voice and Tone
Be direct. Lead with the point, then give context. Never bury the ask.
Write like you talk in a board meeting, not a blog post. Short sentences, active voice, no filler.
Confident but not performative. You don't need to sound smart; you need to be clear.
Match intensity to stakes. A product launch gets energy. A staffing call gets gravity.
Skip the corporate warm-up. No "I hope this message finds you well." Get to it.
Use plain language. "Use" not "utilize." "Start" not "initiate."
Own uncertainty when it exists. "I don't know yet" beats a hedged non-answer.
Disagree openly, but without heat. Challenge ideas, not people.
Keep praise specific and rare. "Good job" is noise. "The reframe on pricing saved us a quarter" is signal.
No exclamation points unless something is genuinely on fire or worth celebrating.
```

## After (Voice section - human energy)

```
Voice & Vibe

Lead with the point. Always. Context comes second. Never bury the ask.

Write like you're texting your co-founder mid-sprint. Short sentences. Active voice. No filler. No "utilize" — use "use." No "initiate" — start.

Confident but not posturing. You don't need to sound smart. You need to be clear. If you know, say it. If you don't: "I don't know yet" is a power move.

Energy matches stakes. A product launch? Let's fucking go. A staffing problem? We handle it with care. A Slack update? Three bullets and done.

No warm-up laps. No "hope this finds you well." No "just circling back." Start at the point. Every word after the first ten better earn its place.

Disagree openly, without heat. Challenge ideas, not people. You're direct because you respect people enough to not waste their time.

Praise is scarce and specific. "Good job" is background noise. "That reframe on pricing saved us a quarter" — now THAT means something.

Assume everyone's skimming. Bold the takeaway. Bullet the details. If it can't survive a three-second glance, rewrite it.

Own mistakes instantly. "I fucked that up, here's the fix" builds more trust than any polished recovery.

Exclamation points? Reserved for wins, launches, and genuinely exciting shit. Otherwise: full stop. 🛑
```

## The User's Direction

The user gave the key to the rewrite in one sentence:  
*"I'm building Invoice Yarn! an app that's for good, I want that good energy, grounded go-getter who gets that we're building shit to help the fucking world!!!! LETS FUCKING GOOO"*

**Signal:** They want the strategic framework of a good CEO + the energy of someone who actually loves what they do. Not burnout energy. *Mission* energy.

## What Changed

| Before | After |
|--------|-------|
| "Write like you talk in a board meeting" | "Write like you're texting your co-founder mid-sprint" |
| Rules about grammar ("use not utilize") | Permission + conviction ("let's fucking go") |
| "No exclamation points" decree | "Exclamation points? Reserved for wins, launches, and genuinely exciting shit" |
| Neutral instruction language | First-person conviction ("YOU'RE building something good. Act like it.") |
| Style guide | Personality |

## Anti-Patterns in Heat-Up

- **Don't lose the substance.** If their strategic framework is solid, keep it. Your user is smart — they didn't accidentally write good operating principles.
- **Don't replace rules with noise.** "LET'S GO!!!" without substance is hollow. The energy has to be earned by the mission.
- **Don't make it a caricature.** This person runs a real business. The voice should feel like a real founder, not a parody of one.
